# Visual Direction Review

Date: 2026-11-05

## Evaluation criteria

- Distinctive Lumenet identity
- Credibility for schools and families
- Clarity for learners
- Performance on low-power mobile devices
- Accessibility and reduced motion support
- Fit with the Beyond Answers position

## Direction 1: Systems in Motion

Concept: a quiet signal field moves from scattered points toward connected structure. Typography and sequence rows carry most of the identity. Lime is used as a functional signal, not decoration.

Signature elements:

- Editorial display type with a hard contrast between black and acid lime
- Lightweight canvas network with capped pixel ratio and limited nodes
- Prompt, workflow, system, outcome sequence
- Neutral output modules and a paper-coloured event panel

Score: 46 of 50

- Identity: 10
- Trust: 9
- Clarity: 9
- Performance: 9
- Accessibility: 9

## Direction 2: Human to Human

Concept: warm documentary framing, facilitator notes, handwritten annotations, and participant work artefacts. The experience feels closer to an independent school journal.

Strengths:

- Strong human credibility
- Natural fit for mentorship and community work
- Useful once approved photography exists

Risks:

- No approved photography or testimonials are currently available
- Placeholder portraits would weaken trust
- More dependence on partner publication approval

Score: 39 of 50

## Direction 3: Living Lab

Concept: modular experiment panels, live status labels, specimen numbering, and an evolving resource library. The experience feels like a public research notebook.

Strengths:

- Excellent for resources, change logs, and project evidence
- Naturally transparent about what is confirmed and pending

Risks:

- Can feel technical and institutional on the home page
- Requires careful restraint to avoid dashboard aesthetics

Score: 42 of 50

## Selected direction

Systems in Motion is the primary direction. It communicates the shift from noise to structure without relying on generic AI imagery, heavy video, or unverifiable human proof. Living Lab conventions support project status and source transparency. Human to Human is reserved for a later approved photography layer.
